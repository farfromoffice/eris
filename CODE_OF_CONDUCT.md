# Code of conduct

## What this covers

Issues, pull requests, reviews, commit messages and anything else that happens
in this repository.

## The short version

Talk about code. Be direct about the code and civil about the person writing it.

## Expected

* Review the change, not the author. "This allocates in an interrupt handler"
  is feedback. "You clearly have no idea how kernels work" is not.
* Say what is wrong and what would be right. A rejection without a reason wastes
  everyone's time.
* Accept that a change can be correct and still not belong in the tree. Layering
  and scope are real arguments.
* Assume the other person read the docs and still got it wrong, rather than that
  they are careless. Then point at the part that was unclear.
* Keep disagreements technical. When two designs both work, the maintainer for
  that area decides and the discussion ends.

## Not accepted

* Insults, harassment, or personal attacks of any kind.
* Comments about someone's identity, background, employer or ability instead of
  their code.
* Sexual language or imagery, unwanted attention, and following someone across
  threads to keep at them.
* Publishing someone's private information.
* Wearing people down by repeating a settled argument in every thread.
* Deliberately unhelpful behaviour: fake reports, spam pull requests, or code
  meant to slip something past review.

Blunt technical criticism is not harassment. Repeated personal criticism is.

## Reporting

Report to the maintainers listed in `MAINTAINERS`. Use a private channel on the
forge: a private security advisory for anything sensitive, or a direct message
to the maintainer of the area involved. Include links to the threads and what
you want to happen.

Reports are read by the maintainers, kept to the people who need to act on them,
and answered. Your identity is not shared with the person reported unless you
agree to it.

## What happens next

Depending on what happened and whether it repeats:

1. A private note explaining what was wrong.
2. A public correction in the thread, and the comment edited or removed.
3. A temporary block from the repository.
4. A permanent ban.

Acting in good faith and apologising once told counts in your favour. Repeating
the same behaviour after a warning does not.

## Scope

This applies to everyone taking part, maintainers included. A maintainer who
breaks these rules answers to the other maintainers, not to nobody.
